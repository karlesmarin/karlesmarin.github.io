Abstract

Kostant's element aP is one point of a line, and this paper is about the rest of it: which points give integer character values, when the character vanishes, and—in type C—what it is when it does not, sign included.

Kostant's 1976 paper singles out two elements of a compact simple group at which every character is 0 or ±1; one of them is aP = ρ(ζt) with t = 2m+2. That element sits on the line θ ↦ exp(2πiθρ♯), and the argument that settles it—an argument about alcoves—needs ρ to be a regular point of the relevant arrangement. At the powers of lower order, aPk with gcd(k, 2m+2) > 1, it is not: ρ moves onto the walls, the argument degenerates, and the values that were 0 and ±1 run to 72 one power later in a group of rank three. Beyond q > 2m no positive root of Cm takes a value above 2ρ1 = 2m, so regularity returns and the line has three regimes rather than two.

Three things are proved.

  1. A classification. Of all rational points g1/q of the line, exactly four families make every character of Sp(2m) an integer: q | 2m, q | 2m+1, q | 2m+2, and a sporadic q = 6 with m ≡ 1 (mod 3). Integrality forces the characteristic polynomial of the natural representation into Z[X], so the spectrum must be stable under the Galois group; classifying the stable spectra is then elementary. The necessity also follows from the field computation of Gannon–Schopieray (2019). The powers of aP are the family q | 2m+2 — one case of a list, not the boundary of what can be said.
  2. A vanishing criterion. With ℓ = λ+ρ and Nq(x) = #{α > 0 : q | (x,α)}, the character is non-zero exactly when Nq(ℓ) = Nq(ρ). The count can never come out below; equality is the whole condition.
  3. The value, in type C, for every power and with its sign: χλ(aPk) = (−1)Ep,q(λ) Zq(λ+ρ)/Zq(ρ) on the surviving weights and 0 elsewhere, with Zq a product of root statistics and Ep,q a sum of floors. That modulus factors as κ(λ) ∈ {1, 2} times a product of dimensions of irreducible representations of GLn, Sp2n and SO2n+1, one per folded class. A closed count of the surviving weights follows for every q, and G2 is settled in closed form at q = 3.

What is not new, stated plainly. At aP itself, nothing here is new. The criterion and its sign are Corollary 3.2 of Cellini–Möseneder Frajria–Papi (2007), in every type and on this very line; read on coroots the count is Corollary 3.7 of Nadimpalli–Pattanayak–Prasad; the principal specialisation the argument runs on is classical; and at q = 2 the factorisation into dimensions has the two-block form of Theorem 5.1 of Karmakar. The paper carries a table that says, line by line, which statement is whose. What is done here is the powers, where ρ is no longer regular — and, in type C, the exact value with its sign, the factorisation into dimensions at q ≥ 3, and the closed count.

What is formalized, and what is not. In Lean 4 with Mathlib (repository karlesmarin/formalizations, commit 6283de8, no sorry, standard axioms only): the type-Cm statements on the root products Fx and their quotient Q — including that Fρ divides Fℓ over any commutative ring — the spectral classification behind the first theorem, and, stated for Q(ζ), the criterion, the value with its sign and the factorisation into dimensions in both parities. Outside Lean: the identification of Q(ζ) with the character (Weyl's formula), the equivalence between spectral and character integrality, that the ν(c) are partitions, and the step from a dominant λ to ℓ. The paper's section “Formalization” gives the scope statement by statement, and the companion site links every statement to its declarations.

Every number is checked, and the checks travel. The floating-point checks of version 1 are replaced by exact certificates (C1–C4: Laurent-polynomial identities, arithmetic in Z[ζq] and the row lemma), each with a control built to fail. Eleven verification gates — on the figures, the formulas, the numbering, the cited attributions, the bibliography, the EN/ES parity and the overlap with the author's own arXiv:2609.02630 — run over the document itself, each with a control. The ancillary archive carries 107 files: the scripts that produce every framed formula and every count, the certificates and the gates, together with their archived output.

Version 2 adds the Lean formalization, the exact certificates, the companion site, and corrections throughout (attributions checked against the sources, the scope of every claim). Included: the paper in English (36 pp) and Spanish (37 pp), and the ancillary archive of scripts and their runs.
