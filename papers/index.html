Maximum number of spanning trees of a multigraph with m edges: exact values for m ≤ 22

Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF
Show moreShow less▾

Let T(m) be the largest number of spanning trees of a connected loopless multigraph with m edges, the number of vertices being free; this is the first question of Problem 2 of R. Kenyon (1996). This record gives T(m) and the complete list of extremal multigraphs, up to isomorphism, for m ≤ 22, together with the maxima S(m) over simple graphs and M(m) over multigraphs with a multiple edge:

T(m) = 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 16, 24, 45, 81, 135, 231, 432, 720, 1280, 2304, 4096, 7168, 12584, 22720, 40960, 72657, 130691 (m = 1, …, 22).

For m ≤ 18 every value and every list of extremizers is backed by a certificate (53 certificates, JSON Lines) accepted by a stand-alone checker that uses only the Python standard library; the completeness of every enumeration in the certificates is checked by the checker's own counting (orbit sums against labelled counts from generating functions), without nauty. For 19 ≤ m ≤ 22 the values come from an exhaustive search with the same proved pruning rules, whose skeleton lists come from nauty; there is no certificate for these four values. From m = 8 on every extremizer is simple with degrees 3 and 4; T(22) is attained only by the circulant C11(1,3). The sequence was not in the OEIS when this record was prepared.

Files: the note (PDF), with a Spanish version (PDF); the values (CSV, JSON); all extremizers of T, S and M with edge lists and multiplicities (JSON, text); the certificates with SHA-256 sums; the checker, an independent re-check of the graph file and the search code (MIT licence), with every pruning rule and its proof and the certificate format; the logs of all checks and controls; a README with the commands that reproduce the checks. The upper bound τ(G)2·8m < 27m and the answer to Kenyon's second question are due to F. Majchrzak (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24523144). I worked with an AI assistant.

How Many Dyck Words Do the Divisors of an Integer Make? Exact Counts and Quasipolynomial Growth

Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

For an integer n ≥ 1, Rodríguez Caballero's word ⟨⟨n⟩⟩₂ records how the divisors of n interleave with the doubled divisors: read D_n △ 2D_n in increasing order and write an opening bracket for elements of D_n and a closing bracket for elements of 2D_n. It is a symmetric Dyck word whose shape reflects classical divisor statistics. Rodríguez Caballero asked which Dyck words arise in this way; we know of no answer in the literature. Let L_k be the set of these words of semilength k. Starting from the reduction to the odd part, we describe the words through the cells of a hyperplane arrangement in the logarithms of the primes, and show that L_k is produced by the single finest factorisation pattern p₁^(q₁−1)⋯p_r^(q_r−1), where k = q₁⋯q_r is the prime factorisation; as a consequence, membership in L is decidable. For the pattern p^a p′^b the number R(a,b) of cells is a classical count: 8R(a,b) − 4a − 4b − 6 is the number of two-dimensional threshold functions on a grid of (2b+1)×(2a+1) points, for which Alekseyev gave an exact formula, so R(a,b) = R(b,a) and its leading coefficient C_a in b is asymptotic to 12a²/π²; we give a direct proof in our coordinates. We show that the number W(a,b) of words satisfies (2/3 − o(1))b² ≤ W(a,b) ≤ (C_a + o(1))b². Together with a theorem of Woods on Presburger counting functions, this shows that for fixed a, W(a,b) is eventually a quasipolynomial in b of degree exactly two; its period and threshold are not determined. We also prove three general mechanisms by which distinct cells give the same word. Finally, |L_2q| = (9q² − 10q + 13)/4 for every odd prime q, with a proof by hand, and |L_3q| = (44q² − 69q + 58 − 3(q/3))/6 for every prime q ≥ 5, where (q/3) = ±1 according as q ≡ ±1 (mod 3). In the second count, one lemma (the listed coincidences are all of them, and are disjoint) is a computer-assisted proof using exact SMT queries (496 queries for completeness and 231 for disjointness, all unsatisfiable, after 38 of 69 branches were discarded as infeasible by the same solver), without a checked proof object. Whether almost every cell has its own word for a ≥ 2 is left to a companion paper.

Version 2 corrects the novelty claim for the cell count R(a,b): it equals (N(2b,2a) + 4a + 4b + 6)/8, where N is the number of two-dimensional threshold functions on a grid (Alekseyev, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 24 (2010); Acketa–Žunić, Inform. Process. Lett. 38 (1991)), which I had overlooked (see also Žunić, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 25 (2011)). It adds Proposition 5.3, which proves this identity in two ways and gives the symmetry R(a,b) = R(b,a), and Remarks 5.4 and 5.5; Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 are kept as a direct proof of a known count. Steps 3 and 5 of Section 6 are worded more precisely, with the same argument. All other statements, proofs and numbers are those of version 1.

Every m×4 biquadratic sum of squares is a sum of 3m−1 squares (5 ≤ m ≤ 16)

Carles Marín Muñoz, Liqun Qi and Chen Ouyang

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

Let BSR(m,n) be the least r such that every real biquadratic sum of squares in m+n variables is a sum of r squares of bilinear forms. We prove BSR(m,4) ≤ 3m−1 for 5 ≤ m ≤ 16, one square below the previous bound 3m. In particular BSR(5,4) ≤ 14, the upper half of the conjecture BSR(5,4) = 14 proposed by two of the present authors, Qi and Ouyang; with the known lower bounds this gives 13 ≤ BSR(5,4) ≤ 14 and 16 ≤ BSR(6,4) ≤ 17. As a corollary, BSR(m,5) ≤ 4m−1 for every m ≥ 2, one below the Choi–Lam–Reznick bound 4m for 5 ≤ m ≤ 10.

The proof studies boundary points of the sets of sums of a fixed number of squares. Minimality of a representation bounds the negative index q of its Hankel multiplier through the dimension of its syzygies, a boundary inertia inequality (a transport to this setting of the negative-index bound of Boumal, Voroninski and Bandeira for Burer–Monteiro critical points) whose case q = 1 recovers the Barvinok–Pataki bound. For m×4 forms it gives q ≤ ⌊(m+13)/6⌋, which is at most 4 exactly for m ≤ 16. A classification of isotropic restrictions involving at most four rows and a low-negative-index lemma for q ≤ 4 then exclude the remaining boundary configurations; compression to fewer rows handles the smaller supports, and an explicit inductive family of tuples gives the regular points. The argument first fails at m = 17.

Rational sums-of-squares lengths of biquadratic forms and orthogonal dilations

Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

For a rational symmetric matrix H of order n with spectrum in [−1, 1], how many squares of rational bilinear forms are needed to write P_H(x, y) = (x₁² + x₂²)|y|² + 2x₁x₂ yᵀHy? Over the reals the answer is n or n + 1 (Blekherman–Plaumann–Sinn–Vinzant, Hanselka–Sinn). Over the rationals it is the least dimension of a sum of squares carrying an orthogonal dilation of a contraction with symmetric part H, and the obstruction is arithmetic: a rotation with rational cosine λ has the real sine √(1 − λ²), and what decides is the imaginary quadratic field ℚ(√(λ² − 1)) of its eigenvalues. For H with one interior eigenvalue we reduce the length to a positive complement inside a sum of squares and decide it by an exact finite local procedure; the excess over n is at most 7, and 7 is attained, first in order 6. Two consequences are explicit: ℓ_ℚ(λI_m) in closed form, governed by the number of rational squares needed for 1 − λ², and the length of the normalized complete graphs (J − I)/(n − 1) for every n, which for odd n is n, n + 3 or n + 5 according to squares and n modulo 8. We also give a criterion over ℚ(t), the integer length of every graph and the smallest gaps between ℝ and ℚ.

On z_2M(21,4): the Hole Extensions of the Chen–Chen Configuration Are Reducible, and the Extremal Configurations of z₂(21,4) Are Not Unique

Carles Marín, Liqun Qi and Gaohang Yu

📕PDF
Show moreShow less▾

The second order extended Zarankiewicz number z_2M(m,n) of Qi and Yu allows a secondary half of a 2-edge to be shared, while the 1-edge graph stays C₄-free and extremal and 1-edges and main halves stay exclusive. Whether z_2M(21,4)=55 is open. Since deleting a 2-edge removes exactly one displayed square, z_2M(21,4)=55 if and only if there is no irreducible admissible configuration with exactly 56 displayed squares, and by the cell count of Qi and Yu and the heredity of irreducibility such a configuration without empty cells is an extremal 55-square configuration of z₂(21,4) plus one 2-edge at its hole. For the extremal configuration of Chen and Chen we show, by certificates verified in exact rational arithmetic, that all 56 such extensions have SOS rank at most 55: 32 by product relations and 24 by contraction certificates of Xu–Yan type. We also show, with computer-assisted certificates and an exhaustive isomorphism search, that the extremal configurations of z₂(21,4) fall into at least six isomorphism classes; Chen and Chen leave the structural classification of extremal augmented graphs open. Finally we prove a six-to-four reduction. For irreducible configurations whose 1-edge graph is C₄-free with m+C(n,2) edges it bounds the multiplicity of a shared half by n(n−1) (12 at 21×4), and it applies directly to an explicit number of the extensions of any extremal configuration of z₂(21,4), eight for that of Chen and Chen, bringing each to at most 54 squares, in agreement with the certificates. The cell count, the heredity lemma, the rows lemma, the six-to-four identity and a 2×2 example are checked in Isabelle/HOL; the remaining arguments are written proofs.

Integer character values on the ρ-line of Sp(2m)

Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

Kostant's element aP is one point of a line, and this paper is about the rest of it: which points give integer character values, when the character vanishes, and—in type C—what it is when it does not, sign included.

Kostant's 1976 paper singles out two elements of a compact simple group at which every character is 0 or ±1; one of them is aP = ρ(ζt) with t = 2m+2. That element sits on the line θ ↦ exp(2πiθρ♯), and the argument that settles it—an argument about alcoves—needs ρ to be a regular point of the relevant arrangement. At the powers of lower order, aPk with gcd(k, 2m+2) > 1, it is not: ρ moves onto the walls, the argument degenerates, and the values that were 0 and ±1 run to 72 one power later in a group of rank three. Beyond q > 2m no positive root of Cm takes a value above 2ρ1 = 2m, so regularity returns and the line has three regimes rather than two.

Three things are proved.

  1. A classification. Of all rational points g1/q of the line, exactly four families make every character of Sp(2m) an integer: q | 2m, q | 2m+1, q | 2m+2, and a sporadic q = 6 with m ≡ 1 (mod 3). Integrality forces the characteristic polynomial of the natural representation into Z[X], so the spectrum must be stable under the Galois group; classifying the stable spectra is then elementary. The necessity also follows from the field computation of Gannon–Schopieray (2019). The powers of aP are the family q | 2m+2 — one case of a list, not the boundary of what can be said.
  2. A vanishing criterion. With ℓ = λ+ρ and Nq(x) = #{α > 0 : q | (x,α)}, the character is non-zero exactly when Nq(ℓ) = Nq(ρ). The count can never come out below; equality is the whole condition.
  3. The value, in type C, for every power and with its sign: χλ(aPk) = (−1)Ep,q(λ) Zq(λ+ρ)/Zq(ρ) on the surviving weights and 0 elsewhere, with Zq a product of root statistics and Ep,q a sum of floors. That modulus factors as κ(λ) ∈ {1, 2} times a product of dimensions of irreducible representations of GLn, Sp2n and SO2n+1, one per folded class. A closed count of the surviving weights follows for every q, and G2 is settled in closed form at q = 3.

What is not new, stated plainly. At aP itself, nothing here is new. The criterion and its sign are Corollary 3.2 of Cellini–Möseneder Frajria–Papi (2007), in every type and on this very line; read on coroots the count is Corollary 3.7 of Nadimpalli–Pattanayak–Prasad; the principal specialisation the argument runs on is classical; and at q = 2 the factorisation into dimensions has the two-block form of Theorem 5.1 of Karmakar. The paper carries a table that says, line by line, which statement is whose. What is done here is the powers, where ρ is no longer regular — and, in type C, the exact value with its sign, the factorisation into dimensions at q ≥ 3, and the closed count.

What is formalized, and what is not. In Lean 4 with Mathlib (repository karlesmarin/formalizations, commit 6283de8, no sorry, standard axioms only): the type-Cm statements on the root products Fx and their quotient Q — including that Fρ divides Fℓ over any commutative ring — the spectral classification behind the first theorem, and, stated for Q(ζ), the criterion, the value with its sign and the factorisation into dimensions in both parities. Outside Lean: the identification of Q(ζ) with the character (Weyl's formula), the equivalence between spectral and character integrality, that the ν(c) are partitions, and the step from a dominant λ to ℓ. The paper's section “Formalization” gives the scope statement by statement, and the companion site links every statement to its declarations.

Every number is checked, and the checks travel. The floating-point checks of version 1 are replaced by exact certificates (C1–C4: Laurent-polynomial identities, arithmetic in Z[ζq] and the row lemma), each with a control built to fail. Eleven verification gates — on the figures, the formulas, the numbering, the cited attributions, the bibliography, the EN/ES parity and the overlap with the author's own arXiv:2609.02630 — run over the document itself, each with a control. The ancillary archive carries 107 files: the scripts that produce every framed formula and every count, the certificates and the gates, together with their archived output.

Version 2 adds the Lean formalization, the exact certificates, the companion site, and corrections throughout (attributions checked against the sources, the scope of every claim). Included: the paper in English (36 pp) and Spanish (37 pp), and the ancillary archive of scripts and their runs.

Let BSR(m,n) be the largest number of squares of real bilinear forms needed to write an m×n biquadratic sum of squares. We prove BSR(4,4) = 11. The lower bound is Qi and Cui's BSR(4,4) ≥ z2A(4,4) = 11; the previous upper bound was 12. The upper bound follows the boundary strategy of Blekherman, Dunbar and Sinn, as in our note on width three: at a hypothetical boundary point of the set of sums of eleven squares inside the cone, a Hankel multiplier of rank at most five satisfies first and second order conditions. For support 3×4, alternating variations parametrized by R3⊗Λ2R4 have a 17-dimensional image in a quadratic space of positive index at most 16. For support 3×3, we use either the width-three trace argument or a syzygy involving the radicals in both factors.

We prove BSR(m,3) = 2m for every m ≥ 2, answering the question of Qi, Löfberg and Chen on the exact values of BSR(m,3); in particular BSR(4,3) = 8, which closes the interval 8 ≤ BSR(4,3) ≤ 9. Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

The limited augmented Zarankiewicz number z_L(m,n) of Qi, Cui and Xu bounds the maximum sum-of-squares rank of m× n biquadratic forms from below: it counts the cells of a maximum C₄-free set in the m× n grid together with pairs of further cells that avoid two forbidden patterns. We determine z_L(m,3)=⌊3m/2⌋+2 for every m≥3, answering a question of Qi, Löfberg and Chen, with a proof formalized in Lean; it also shows that the gap to their weak variant grows like m/6. For a fixed number n of columns we prove that each new row adds ρₙ pairs up to a bounded error, where ρₙ is a rational number given by finitely many linear programs, and that the error is eventually periodic. Pairs placed around a hollow triangle of three column blocks give ρₙ≥λₙ=n/3−1/2+O(1/n); balanced blocks are optimal within this family, and ρₙ=λₙ for n≤6 by rational dual certificates checked in exact arithmetic; we conjecture equality for all n. Consequently, in every fixed width the original criterion stays linearly below the recursive-line, signed and second-order ones. We also reduce Lebedev's canonical base to a graph parameter whose sharp bound is due to Hof, which gives its slope ⌊(n−1)²/4⌋/(n−1) for every n; we find the exact value of z_L(m,4) for every m≥6 by certified computation, fourteen new exact values by exhaustive computer search (eight of them instances of the three- and four-column formulas), and a three-dimensional analogue in which the naive bound of one quarter fails already in 3×3×5, the smallest such box. The admissibility lemmas behind the constructions are formalized in Isabelle/HOL.

Integer frieze patterns and Tabachnikov's four sign changes

Carles Marín Muñoz

📕PDF📘Blueprint
Show moreShow less▾

Tabachnikov asked for which rows the differences between the entries of a row of two frieze patterns of the same width must change sign at least four times along the period, and left open whether this holds for all rows when the entries are positive integers. We show that it does not. For every even n ≥ 14 there are two integer (Conway--Coxeter) frieze patterns of width n − 3 whose middle rows are strictly ordered, and for every odd n ≥ 15 there are two whose rows ⌊(n−3)/2⌋ differ with no zero and exactly two sign changes.
