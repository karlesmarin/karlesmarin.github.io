Abstract

For an integer n ≥ 1, Rodríguez Caballero's word ⟨⟨n⟩⟩₂ records how the divisors of n interleave with the doubled divisors: read D_n △ 2D_n in increasing order and write an opening bracket for elements of D_n and a closing bracket for elements of 2D_n. It is a symmetric Dyck word whose shape reflects classical divisor statistics. Rodríguez Caballero asked which Dyck words arise in this way; we know of no answer in the literature. Let L_k be the set of these words of semilength k. Starting from the reduction to the odd part, we describe the words through the cells of a hyperplane arrangement in the logarithms of the primes, and show that L_k is produced by the single finest factorisation pattern p₁^(q₁−1)⋯p_r^(q_r−1), where k = q₁⋯q_r is the prime factorisation; as a consequence, membership in L is decidable. For the pattern p^a p′^b we give an exact formula for the number R(a,b) of cells, whose leading coefficient C_a is asymptotic to 12a²/π², and show that the number W(a,b) of words satisfies (2/3 − o(1))b² ≤ W(a,b) ≤ (C_a + o(1))b². Together with a theorem of Woods on Presburger counting functions, this shows that for fixed a, W(a,b) is eventually a quasipolynomial in b of degree exactly two; its period and threshold are not determined. We also prove three general mechanisms by which distinct cells give the same word. Finally, |L_2q| = (9q² − 10q + 13)/4 for every odd prime q, with a proof by hand, and |L_3q| = (44q² − 69q + 58 − 3(q/3))/6 for every prime q ≥ 5, where (q/3) = ±1 according as q ≡ ±1 (mod 3). In the second count, one lemma (the listed coincidences are all of them, and are disjoint) is a computer-assisted proof using exact SMT queries (496/496 unsatisfiable), without a checked proof object. Whether almost every cell has its own word for a ≥ 2 is left to a companion paper.

Files: the paper in English and in Spanish; Supplement B (the hand proofs of the coincidence families behind |L_3q| and the documentation of the SMT step); its package of scripts, each run with its output, and the recorded logs of the full solver runs; the SMT-LIB2 files of the 496 direct queries; the LaTeX sources; and an offline copy of the companion site. Some steps are checked in Isabelle/HOL (https://github.com/karlesmarin/formalizations, folder Isabelle/CaballeroDyckWords): the reduction to the odd part and the Dyck property, the mixed-radix bijection behind the refinement theorem, the closing count of |L_2q|, the algebraic identities of the event encoding, and part of the link with the Kassel–Reutenauer polynomials. These checks cover individual steps, not the theorems as a whole. The companion site https://karlesmarin.github.io/blueprints/dyck-divisors/ says, for each statement, what is formalized and what is checked by computation. I worked with an AI assistant.
