Instability order and the selection mechanism of self-similar blowup in the Sabra shell model, and a family-type dichotomy of blowup universality

Carles Marín Muñoz

2026-05-25DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20385792CC BY 4.0

🌀 Turbulence & shell models

📕PDF🔗DOI🗄️Zenodo🇪🇸Español

Abstract

The inviscid Sabra shell model of turbulence blows up in finite time through a self-similar profile recently shown to be non-unique: an infinite family of N-bump profiles coexists with the attracting one-bump profile, leaving open (i) the linear stability of the family and (ii) the selection mechanism that makes the one-bump profile universal (Campolina-Simonnet-Thalabard, 2025). We compute the one-bump profile to machine precision, derive its renormalized-time stability operator, and show its spectrum is stable (instability order 0) up to the trivial t*-shift, translation, and a golden-ratio phase mode arising from the Sabra phase symmetry (a Fibonacci recurrence). Treating the N-bump profile as a weakly-coupled bound state of one-bump pulses, we show by a pulse-interaction computation that its instability order equals N-1: only the one-bump profile is a stable attractor, which explains the observed selection. We then test the recently proposed rate-instability-order universality law and find it does not transfer to Sabra: its exponents cluster (multi-pulse accumulation) rather than spread (reciprocal-affine). This motivates a family-type dichotomy of self-similar blowup - distinct-profile / intrinsic-instability families (rate spreads) versus multi-pulse / interaction families (rate clusters) - with a sharp discriminator: the internal phase freedom of the pulse.
