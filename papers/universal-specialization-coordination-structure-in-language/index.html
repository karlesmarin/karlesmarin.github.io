Universal Specialization-Coordination Structure in Language Model Training

Carles Marín Muñoz

2026-03-24DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19199838CC BY 4.0

🤖 Transformers & LLM

📕PDF🔗DOI🗄️Zenodo🇪🇸Español

Abstract

  We report a universal organizational structure governing how transformer
  language models train: outer layers specialize while inner layers coordinate,
  and this reorganization follows a strict temporal order that precedes any
  measurable change in model performance.

  Across Pythia-70M, 160M, and 410M (6, 12, and 24 layers; 35 checkpoints
  each, up to step 143K), we show: (1) outer layer pairs develop increasingly
  distinct representations as capabilities emerge (inter-layer CKA falls
  monotonically, r = −0.93 to −0.96 with LAMBADA accuracy); (2) inner layer
  pairs converge to CKA ≈ 1.0, forming a permanent coordination zone whose
  fractional depth scales as 1−2/N (33% → 58% → 67% for N = 6, 12, 24);
  (3) an informational leading indicator Ob = I(X;Y)/H(Y), estimated via
  Gaussian MI with PCA reduction, peaks 500–1000 steps before the CKA
  transition, which precedes benchmark improvement — establishing the temporal
  chain Ob-peak → CKA reorganization → capability.

  This same chain was previously observed in 2-layer transformers during
  grokking on modular arithmetic [Marín, 2026]. Its replication in large
  language models suggests it is a fundamental property of transformer
  training, not an artifact of small-scale tasks.

  Practical implication: Ob provides a leading diagnostic for capability
  emergence that requires no benchmarks, no knowledge of the learned
  algorithm, and only forward passes through the model. A 72-configuration
  hyperparameter sweep (6 values of β2, 6 warmup fractions, 2 learning rates)
  confirms that the filter based on early Ob onset correctly identifies all 5
  non-grokking configurations (5/5, 100% precision) before 5% of the training
  budget is spent.
